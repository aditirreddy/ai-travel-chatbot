JAMAICA FOOD-Jamaica is a foodie paradise known for bold and vibrant flavors. Jamaican cuisine is a fusion of culinary techniques, flavors, and spices influenced by the island's Taino, African, Irish, English, French, Portuguese, Spanish, Indian, Chinese, and Middle Eastern heritage. Jamaica offers everything from famous street foods to fine dining and authentic Jamaican dishes.

ACKEE AND SALTFISH-Ackee and Saltfish is Jamaica's national dish and a popular local breakfast. It is often served with fried dumplings, callaloo, green bananas, avocado, and plantains, depending on what is in season.

JAMAICAN FAVORITES-Other popular Jamaican dishes include oxtail, stew peas, curry goat, and rice and peas.

JERK FOOD-Jamaica is synonymous with jerk food, which includes smoky, pimento-wood-flavored meats, seafood, and vegetarian dishes. Jerk food is served at restaurants and food stands throughout the island. Scotch Bonnet peppers are commonly used in Jamaican cooking and are flavorful but very hot. Popular places to try jerk include Scotchies in St. Ann, Boston Bay in Portland, and Pepper Thyme in Kingston.

SEAFOOD-Jamaica's abundant coastline makes it a great destination for seafood lovers. Popular seafood dishes include fried fish and festival at Hellshire and steamed fish with okra and bammy. Seafood can be enjoyed on the beach or at waterfront restaurants.

MANNISH WATER-Mannish water is a traditional Jamaican dish that can be spiked with white rum. Trying it is described as an adventurous way to experience Jamaican cuisine and earn bragging rights toward a "Jamaican Passport."

INTERNATIONAL FOOD-Jamaica offers more than traditional Jamaican food. Visitors can easily find Chinese, Indian, Syrian, and other international cuisine options throughout the island.

ITAL FOOD-Ital is a traditional diet practiced by Jamaican Rastafarians for vitality, energy, and life force. Jamaica offers many fresh options for vegan and vegetarian travelers, including farm-to-table foods.

TROPICAL FRUITS-Jamaica's tropical climate produces a wide variety of exotic fruits. Visitors can enjoy mangoes, pineapples, papayas, starfruit, and guava.

JAMAICAN DRINKS-Jamaica offers a variety of refreshing tropical drinks. Coconut water can be enjoyed straight from the source, while freshly squeezed limeade provides a tangy option. Sorrel is a traditional hibiscus-based drink commonly enjoyed during the holidays, and ginger beer is another popular Jamaican drink.

JAMAICAN DESSERTS-Jamaicans enjoy sweets and traditional desserts such as sweet potato pudding. Sweet potato pudding is cooked under a heap of hot coals and is sometimes described by locals as "Hell a top, Hell a bottom, and Hallelujah in de middle."

THE PUDDING MAN-The Pudding Man, also known as Jus Coool, is located in Priory, St. Ann and is recommended for trying Jamaican sweet potato pudding.

JAMAICA BLUE MOUNTAIN COFFEE-Jamaica Blue Mountain coffee is described as one of the world's finest coffees and can be enjoyed with traditional Jamaican desserts such as sweet potato pudding.
