PUERTO RICO - Puerto Rico is a place where every corner tells a story. You can begin your day beneath the lush canopy of a tropical rainforest and end it watching the sunset from a centuries-old fortress. The Island is a vibrant tapestry of color, including historic facades, turquoise coastlines and landscapes built for discovery. For those who crave excitement, Puerto Rico offers adventures ranging from flying across some of the world’s longest ziplines to trekking through limestone caves and surfing world-class breaks before noon. At its core, the Island moves to a rhythm that can be felt in local markets and tasted in the food, such as slow-roasted pernil shared among friends. Puerto Rican culture is rooted in warmth, celebration and belonging, creating an energy that makes the experience personal. Puerto Rico also has a strong artisan tradition, with skilled local creators preserving cultural legacies through sustainable craftsmanship. Artisans create items such as masks carved from higüera (calabash) wood and hand-stitched leather goods, with each piece reflecting Puerto Rican identity, heritage and care for the environment. Visitors can meet artisans, discover the eco-friendly resources they use and learn how their work is rooted in Boricua tradition.

PUERTO RICAN ARTISANS - In Puerto Rico, artisans, known locally as artesanos, are cultural custodians who preserve and celebrate the Island’s rich heritage through handcrafted works. Their creations are deeply rooted in Taíno, African and Spanish traditions, reflecting the diverse influences that shape Puerto Rican identity. Every handcrafted piece reflects Puerto Rico’s creativity and culture. Artisans use materials including wood, clay, bamboo, leather, cotton thread, natural fibers, sea glass, wax, shells, seeds, spices, stone, metal, paper, glass, higüeras, textiles, tobacco and other natural elements sourced from Puerto Rico’s landscapes and coastlines. They transform everyday materials into works of art, including colorful vejigante masks, carved santos, handmade jewelry and delicate mundillo lacework, with each craft helping preserve the Island’s cultural heritage.

HIGÜERA - Higüera, also known as calabash, is a native fruit that is dried, hollowed and sanded to create natural containers and surfaces. Puerto Rican artisans transform higüera into hand-painted art, decorative masks and musical instruments, celebrating its long-standing use by Taíno ancestors. Higüera is also used to create prints and other decorative artwork.

GUAYACÁN - Guayacán is a dense and durable tropical hardwood native to the Caribbean and is considered one of the strongest woods in the world. Its golden-brown wood has been valued for centuries in Puerto Rican craftsmanship and is often used for religious carvings, tools and ceremonial objects because of its strength and symbolic meaning.

COTTON AND MUNDILLO - Puerto Rico is known for its tradition of mundillo, a delicate bobbin lace-making craft passed down through generations of artisans. Using cotton thread, wooden bobbins and intricate patterns, artisans create detailed lace pieces for clothing, home décor, accessories and decorative art. The tradition remains especially important in Moca, known as the Capital del Mundillo, where local artisans continue to preserve and celebrate this cherished craft.

COCONUT - Coconut shell is commonly used in Puerto Rican artisan work. The shell is a byproduct of the fruit that can be cleaned, polished and carved into jewelry, buttons, home décor and even traditional vejigante masks. This sustainable material embodies the resourceful spirit of island life by transforming everyday natural waste into lasting pieces of art.

LEATHER - Leather has long been used by Puerto Rican artisans and is traditionally crafted from treated hides. Artisans create belts, handbags, wallets and decorative pieces using carving, tooling and stitching techniques passed down through generations. Leather’s durability and versatility allow artisans to showcase detailed craftsmanship.

VEJIGANTE MASKS - Bright, colorful masks used in traditional festivals in Loíza, Ponce and Hatillo, vejigante masks are among Puerto Rico’s most recognized cultural symbols. Their bold colors and playful horns make them distinctive representations of Puerto Rican culture and celebration.

WOOD CARVINGS AND SANTOS - Wood carvings are hand-carved figures often made from local wood. Many represent saints, while others celebrate everyday Island life and Puerto Rican traditions. Santos are an important form of traditional Puerto Rican craftsmanship.

HIGÜERA ART - Higüera art includes decorative pieces, musical instruments and other crafts made from dried higüera gourds. Each piece is carefully shaped, carved and painted by hand.

PUERTO RICAN ARTISAN JEWELRY - Puerto Rican artisans create handmade necklaces, bracelets, earrings and rings using sea glass, shells, seeds, metals and other natural materials found around the Island. These pieces allow visitors to carry a piece of Puerto Rico wherever they go.

PUERTO RICAN LEATHERWORK - Traditional artisans create belts, handbags, wallets and other accessories using time-honored leatherworking techniques. The craft combines durability with detailed carving, tooling and stitching.

PUERTO RICAN TEXTILES AND EMBROIDERY - Puerto Rican textile crafts include handwoven fabrics, embroidered designs and clothing inspired by Puerto Rican heritage, colors and patterns. These crafts help preserve traditional visual styles while adapting them into useful and decorative items.

PUERTO RICAN CERAMICS AND POTTERY - Puerto Rican artisans create functional and decorative ceramic and pottery pieces by hand. Designs are often inspired by Taíno heritage and contemporary Puerto Rican art.

PUERTO RICAN ARTISAN MARKETS AND FESTIVALS - Throughout the year, artisan fairs and cultural festivals across Puerto Rico bring together local makers, traditional crafts, live music and authentic food. These events give visitors opportunities to meet artisans, learn about their techniques and purchase handcrafted items directly from creators. Supporting local artisans helps preserve Boricua cultural identity. The artisan market at Paseo de la Princesa in Old San Juan takes place regularly throughout the year and offers handmade jewelry, vejigante masks, wood carvings, textiles and other locally crafted goods while visitors explore the historic streets.

FERIA NACIONAL DE ARTESANÍAS DE BARRANQUITAS - Feria Nacional de Artesanías de Barranquitas is a longstanding annual artisan festival showcasing the work of artisans from across Puerto Rico. It is one of the notable events celebrating traditional craftsmanship, music, food and culture.

SOMOS COLOR CARIBE - Somos Color Caribe in Dorado highlights Caribbean art, culture, music and artisan exhibits as part of Puerto Rico’s artisan and cultural event calendar.

LA FERIA EN CATAÑO - La Feria en Cataño, also known as Feria Bacardí, is a waterfront celebration featuring local artisans, traditional foods, live entertainment and family-friendly activities.

YARIELIS SEVILLA VELÁZQUEZ - Yarielis Sevilla Velázquez is a Puerto Rican artisan from Vega Baja known for vibrant vejigante masks and cultural prints using higüera gourds. She has been an artisan for 12 years and is a visual artist whose work has appeared in films, commercials and music videos. She showcases her work throughout the Island, especially in Old San Juan with Puerto Rico’s Tourism Company and the Institute of Art and Culture. She also teaches art and helps develop the next generation of creators through higüeras and vejigante masks.

PUERTO RICO 7-DAY ITINERARY - A one-week Puerto Rico itinerary takes visitors from vibrant city streets to lush rainforests, coastal hideaways and cultural gems across the Island. Whether it is a first trip or a return visit, the itinerary includes Puerto Rican flavors, sights and adventures. For exploring beyond San Juan, renting a car is highly recommended. Visitors should call ahead for reservations and opening hours at popular attractions. The itinerary covers when to go and what to pack, Days 1 and 2 in Old San Juan, Day 3 in El Yunque Rainforest, Days 4 and 5 in the West Region, Day 6 in Ponce and Day 7 for a final sunrise and meal.

PUERTO RICO 7-DAY ITINERARY - WHEN TO GO - The ideal time for a seven-day Puerto Rico itinerary is between December and April, when the weather is generally warm and dry. Travelers seeking fewer crowds can consider May or November, while the weather remains pleasant year-round. Hurricane season runs from June through November and is described as a period best avoided for a more relaxing trip.

PUERTO RICO 7-DAY ITINERARY - WHAT TO PACK - Bring light, breathable clothing for daytime exploration and a swimsuit for beach and water activities. Comfortable shoes are important for walking through historic streets and hiking nature trails. Sunscreen and sunglasses help protect against the sun, while bug repellent is useful for rainforest adventures. A light jacket or sweater can be useful for cooler evenings, especially in mountainous areas.

OLD SAN JUAN - Old San Juan is a mandatory stop for visitors and forms the focus of Days 1 and 2 of the seven-day itinerary. The area offers colorful streets, historic landmarks, vibrant nightlife, history, art and local flavor. Must-see sights include Castillo San Felipe del Morro, a 500-year-old Spanish fort offering panoramic coastline views; El Cuartel de Ballajá; Museo de Las Américas; the Gate of San Juan; and La Rogativa. These locations also provide excellent photo opportunities.

CASTILLO SAN FELIPE DEL MORRO - Castillo San Felipe del Morro is a 500-year-old Spanish fort in Old San Juan offering panoramic views of the coastline. It is one of the essential historic landmarks to visit during a Puerto Rico itinerary.

EL CUARTEL DE BALLAJÁ - El Cuartel de Ballajá is a historic landmark in Old San Juan and one of the recommended sights to visit during a seven-day Puerto Rico itinerary.

MUSEO DE LAS AMÉRICAS - Museo de Las Américas in Old San Juan is recommended for learning about Puerto Rico’s rich history and cultural heritage.

GATE OF SAN JUAN - The Gate of San Juan is a historic landmark in Old San Juan and a recommended stop for visitors exploring the city. It also provides a good photo opportunity.

LA ROGATIVA - La Rogativa is a historic landmark in Old San Juan and a recommended photo stop during exploration of the historic district.

SAN JUAN DINING - Puerto Rican food is a central part of the Old San Juan experience. Barrachina is known for its legendary piña colada and traditional Puerto Rican fare. Chocobar offers a twist on local favorites with chocolate-infused dishes, while Pirilo is known for artisan pizzas. El Jibarito and Café Manolín provide casual, hearty meals reflecting Puerto Rican culinary traditions.

BARRACHINA - Barrachina in San Juan is known for its legendary piña colada and traditional Puerto Rican food and is recommended as a dining stop during an Old San Juan itinerary.

CHOCOBAR - Chocobar in San Juan offers a creative twist on local favorites through chocolate-infused dishes and is recommended for visitors looking for a distinctive dining experience.

PIRILO - Pirilo in San Juan is recommended for artisan pizzas and provides a different take on local dining during an Old San Juan visit.

EL JIBARITO - El Jibarito in San Juan is a casual restaurant offering hearty meals that reflect Puerto Rican culinary traditions.

CAFÉ MANOLÍN - Café Manolín in San Juan is a casual dining option offering hearty food inspired by Puerto Rican culinary traditions.

CASA BACARDÍ - Casa BACARDÍ in Cataño is recommended for a rum tasting tour or mixology class. Visitors can learn how to make cocktails and can even personalize a bottle of BACARDÍ as a unique Puerto Rico souvenir.

LA PLACITA DE SANTURCE - La Placita de Santurce is a bustling marketplace by day and a lively social hub by night. It is recommended for experiencing San Juan’s nightlife after a day of sightseeing.

SAN JUAN NIGHTLIFE - San Juan offers nightlife options including La Placita de Santurce, Fifty-Eight and the San Juan Marriott lobby bar. La Placita functions as a marketplace during the day and social hub at night, while Fifty-Eight and the San Juan Marriott offer clubbing, live music and dancing.

EL YUNQUE NATIONAL FOREST - El Yunque National Forest is a must-visit destination on a Puerto Rico itinerary and is located just under an hour from San Juan. The lush rainforest features scenic trails, waterfalls and panoramic views not found elsewhere in the United States. Visitors can begin at the El Portal Visitor Center to gather maps and information about the forest’s biodiversity. Popular trails include the Mt. Britton Trail, which leads to a stone tower with panoramic views, and the Big Tree Trail, which leads to La Mina Falls. The more challenging El Yunque Trail ascends toward the forest’s peak and provides breathtaking vistas. Weather changes quickly, so visitors should expect rain even on sunny days. Comfortable hiking shoes, water, sunscreen, bug spray and a light rain jacket are recommended. Visitors should remain on marked trails and never leave trash behind. El Yunque can be combined with a stop at the Luquillo kiosks for beachside food or a swim in natural pools along the La Coca Trail when conditions permit.

EL PORTAL VISITOR CENTER - El Portal Visitor Center in El Yunque National Forest is a useful starting point where visitors can gather maps and information about the rainforest’s biodiversity before hiking.

MT. BRITTON TRAIL - Mt. Britton Trail in El Yunque leads hikers to a stone tower with panoramic views of the rainforest and surrounding landscape.

BIG TREE TRAIL - Big Tree Trail in El Yunque leads to the picturesque La Mina Falls and is one of the popular hiking routes in the rainforest.

LA MINA FALLS - La Mina Falls is a picturesque waterfall reached via the Big Tree Trail in El Yunque National Forest.

EL YUNQUE TRAIL - El Yunque Trail is a more challenging rainforest hike that ascends toward the forest’s peak and offers breathtaking vistas.

LUQUILLO KIOSKS - The Luquillo kiosks are a recommended stop when visiting El Yunque, offering beachside food and a chance to combine rainforest exploration with local dining near the coast.

LA COCA TRAIL NATURAL POOLS - The natural pools along the La Coca Trail can provide an opportunity for a quick swim after exploring El Yunque, provided conditions are safe and suitable.

WEST REGION OF PUERTO RICO - Days 4 and 5 of the itinerary focus on the West region, where the pace slows, sunsets glow and waves roll in. The area is known for surf towns, scenic drives and a laid-back atmosphere. It provides an opportunity to explore beyond San Juan and experience Puerto Rico’s natural beauty. Recommended stops include Río Camuy Cave Park, Túnel de Guajataca and Cara del Indio, followed by beaches and activities in Rincón.

RÍO CAMUY CAVE PARK - Río Camuy Cave Park is a massive cave system carved by the third-largest underground river in the world. It is recommended as a scenic stop when driving west from San Juan and provides opportunities to learn about Puerto Rico’s geology, Taíno heritage and lush interior landscape.

TÚNEL DE GUAJATACA - Túnel de Guajataca is a historic railroad tunnel that opens toward ocean views and a dramatic turquoise beach. The former railway tunnel is recommended as a scenic stop when traveling west from San Juan and is also noted for its beautiful surrounding walking trail, murals and coastline.

CARA DEL INDIO - Cara del Indio is a dramatic rock carving honoring Taíno chief Mabodamaca. It is recommended as a quick but memorable stop when traveling toward Rincón and serves as a cultural and scenic landmark.

RINCON - Rincón is a laid-back beach town on Puerto Rico’s west coast known for excellent beaches, surfing, scenic sunsets and a relaxed atmosphere. Domes Beach is ideal for experienced surfers, while Sandy Beach and Steps Beach are recommended for swimming, snorkeling and sunbathing. Visitors can also paddleboard in calm waters or try a morning yoga class at Barefoot Yoga. Recommended attractions and activities include Mirador de Guajataca, Punta Higüera Lighthouse Park, Secret Garden Art Gallery, horseback riding and surf lessons through local outfitters such as Rincón Surfing School or Sail Rincón.

DOMES BEACH - Domes Beach in Rincón is recommended for experienced surfers and is one of the area’s notable beaches.

SANDY BEACH - Sandy Beach in Rincón is recommended for swimming, snorkeling and sunbathing.

STEPS BEACH - Steps Beach in Rincón is recommended for swimming, snorkeling and relaxing on the beach.

BAREFOOT YOGA - Barefoot Yoga in Rincón offers morning yoga classes and is recommended as a relaxing activity during a west coast visit.

MIRADOR DE GUAJATACA - Mirador de Guajataca is a scenic viewpoint recommended as a quick stop on the way to Rincón. It offers one of the most breathtaking views of the Atlantic Ocean.

PUNTA HIGÜERA LIGHTHOUSE PARK - Punta Higüera Lighthouse Park in Rincón is recommended for watching the sun set over the Atlantic Ocean. Visitors may also catch a glimpse of whales during winter.

SECRET GARDEN ART GALLERY - Secret Garden Art Gallery in Rincón is recommended for art lovers exploring the town.

RINCON SURFING SCHOOL - Rincón Surfing School is a local outfitter through which visitors can book surf lessons in Rincón.

SAIL RINCÓN - Sail Rincón is a local outfitter offering activities such as horseback rides or surf-related experiences in the Rincón area.

RINCON DINING - The English Rose is a hilltop favorite offering panoramic views and a hearty breakfast menu. For dinner, La Copa Llena and Tamboo Beside the Pointe are recommended for beachside dining, while Cowboys Cantina offers steak and rodeo-style atmosphere. Rincón provides a variety of casual and scenic dining options.

LA COPA LLENA - La Copa Llena in Rincón is recommended for beachside dining during an evening meal.

RINCON OF THE SEAS - Rincón of the Seas is a boutique resort option in Rincón and is recommended as one of the lodging choices for visitors exploring the west coast.

VILLA COFRESÍ - Villa Cofresí is a boutique lodging option in Rincón and is recommended as a place to stay near the beach.

PONCE - Ponce, known as La Perla del Sur, or the Pearl of the South, is located on Puerto Rico’s southern coast and offers a different atmosphere from San Juan and Rincón. The city combines rich history, vibrant art, classic Caribbean charm, neoclassical architecture, rum heritage and an active arts scene centered around a charming town square. Day 6 of the seven-day itinerary focuses on Ponce. Plaza Las Delicias is the heart of the city and contains Parque de Bombas and the Cathedral of Our Lady of Guadalupe. Other highlights include Castillo Serrallés, Museo de Arte de Ponce, colorful street art from Ponce es Ley, King’s Cream and La Guancha. Dining options include La Cava Restaurant, Campioni Pizza Birra & Tapas and seaside kiosks.

PLAZA LAS DELICIAS - Plaza Las Delicias is the heart of Ponce and contains major landmarks including Parque de Bombas and the Cathedral of Our Lady of Guadalupe. It is a central location for exploring the city’s historic and cultural attractions.

PARQUE DE BOMBAS - Parque de Bombas is a historic firehouse in Ponce that has been converted into a museum. Its bold red-and-black exterior makes it one of the city’s most recognizable landmarks.

CATHEDRAL OF OUR LADY OF GUADALUPE - The Cathedral of Our Lady of Guadalupe in Ponce is a major cultural and religious landmark located around Plaza Las Delicias. It is surrounded by lion statues and fountains.

CASTILLO SERRALLÉS - Castillo Serrallés is a hilltop mansion in Ponce with gardens and panoramic views. It was once home to the family behind Don Q Rum and is a recommended cultural landmark.

MUSEO DE ARTE DE PONCE - Museo de Arte de Ponce houses more than 4,000 works of art, including Flaming June by Lord Frederic Leighton. It is a major cultural attraction and is recommended for art lovers visiting Ponce.

PONCE ES LEY - Ponce es Ley is an annual urban art festival that has left colorful murals throughout downtown Ponce. Visitors can stroll through the city to admire the murals and street art.

KING'S CREAM - King’s Cream is a local Ponce favorite for tropical ice cream and is recommended as a refreshing stop while exploring the city.

LA GUANCHA - La Guancha is a seaside boardwalk in Ponce offering views of the Caribbean Sea, casual food, kiosks and opportunities for people-watching. It is recommended for an evening or afternoon stop after exploring the city.

PONCE DINING - La Cava Restaurant is recommended for upscale comida criolla with a twist. Campioni Pizza Birra & Tapas combines local ingredients with Mediterranean flavors for Italian-inspired food. Along the pier, casual kiosks offer classic fritters and cold drinks with ocean views.

LA CAVA RESTAURANT - La Cava Restaurant in Ponce is recommended for upscale comida criolla with a modern twist.

CAMPIONI PIZZA BIRRA & TAPAS - Campioni Pizza Birra & Tapas in Ponce combines local ingredients with Mediterranean influences and is recommended for Italian-style food.

PUERTO RICO DAY 7 - The seventh and final day is designed for slowing down and enjoying the last moments of island life. Travelers flying from Ponce can depart through La Mercedita Airport (PSE), while those returning to San Juan can fly from Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport (SJU). The morning can begin with breakfast at a local café, followed by a final walk along La Guancha or one of Rincón’s beaches. The overall message is to savor the final sunrise, meal and ocean views before leaving Puerto Rico, with the reminder that the Island has a way of staying with visitors and inspiring a future return.

PANADERÍA ESPAÑA - Panadería España in Ponce is recommended as a breakfast option on the final morning of a seven-day Puerto Rico trip.

CAFÉ DON JUAN - Café Don Juan is recommended as a breakfast option if travelers are back in the San Juan metropolitan area on the final morning.

LUIS MUÑOZ MARÍN INTERNATIONAL AIRPORT - Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport (SJU) in the San Juan metropolitan area is the recommended departure airport for travelers returning to San Juan for their flight home.
